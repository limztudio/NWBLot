# Font atlas measurement and quality report

This report preserves the recorded atlas qualification, footprint comparison and UASTC trial from the source snapshot of the [font-atlas guide](../font_atlas_plan.md). These observations belong to the stated shipped assets and comparison conditions; they are retained evidence, not a claim of a new run or a general qualification of other fonts, magnifications or platforms.

## Recorded scale and outline quality qualification

In the fixed internal FreeType scalar SDF encoding, byte 128 represents zero distance and positive distance is inside the glyph. The shader chooses one channel and reconstructs `(sample * 255 - 128) * spread / 128`, using derivatives to adjust coverage. The qualified SDF interval is **0.75..1.5 times the bake ppem** in physical pixels; outside it, the same selected face uses native grayscale coverage. The default 32-ppem Latin and Korean atlases therefore serve 24..48 physical pixels per em. Below that interval, native coverage preserves the antialiased bottom row of small descenders. Recorded qualification compared Latin `A`/`o`/`e`/`g`/`q` and Korean `한` at 48 glyph/scale combinations within this interval, requiring at most one pixel of edge displacement and mean coverage error no greater than 0.04. The former quality unit target is retired; these figures remain the historical qualification for the shipped atlases. Larger magnification failed at sharp corners, so this distance representation does not promise unlimited zoom.

## Compact shipped-image footprint comparison

The shipped faces bake at 32 ppem with spread 8. Latin stores 1024 × 1024 RGBA8 and 1024 × 1023 RG8 groups, totaling 6,289,408 image bytes (5.998046875 MiB). Korean stores two 2048 × 2048 RGBA8 groups, one 2048 × 2047 RGBA8 group and one 2048 × 1209 R8 group, totaling 52,799,488 bytes (50.353515625 MiB). Combined image data is 56.3515625 MiB, a reduction of 15.6484375 MiB (21.733941%) from the previous 72 MiB. Actual GPU allocations can include alignment and tiling overhead; the defaults contain no three-channel group requiring the RGB8 upload expansion.

## Historical UASTC trial

Before channel and rectangle compaction, the conditional UASTC preference was tested against the then-shipped four-channel atlases. The pinned LDR `tex_conv --linear` encoder compressed block-aligned 96x96 crops from the actual RGBA pages; the pinned Basis decoder reconstructed their first mip. A probe selected each glyph's assigned channel and compared bilinear SDF coverage with the lossless page at 24, 32, and 48 physical pixels per em. The sample included Latin `A`, `o`, `e`, `i`, `m`, `g`, `q` and Korean `한`, `가`.

The compressed `g` and `q` edges moved up to **2 pixels** at 48 pixels per em; `A` and `한` also reached 2 pixels. At the stored distance threshold, `g` changed sign in 43 of 1,344 glyph texels and `한` in 69 of 1,980. The largest additional mean coverage error against the lossless page was 0.0306 (`한` at 32 pixels per em). This representative comparison did not repeat the native-outline qualification, but it already exceeds the current one-pixel edge budget relative to the lossless atlas. The format therefore retains exact SDF bytes. At that time, directly sampled UASTC could have reduced the raw 8 MiB Latin and 64 MiB Korean payloads to 2 and 16 MiB; those figures describe the previous four-channel layout. A compression strategy for the current compact groups needs its own native-outline quality gate before changing the format.

## Recorded verification context

The output integration suite exercises complete Latin and Korean generation, FON2 source decoding, bunch expansion and both metadata importers, reconstructed runtime FTA1 version 2 decoding, all-glyph coverage and exact f32 metric bits, compact group bounds and guards, non-power-of-two capacity, exact source positioning bytes, deterministic repetition, raw-SFNT and prepared-font equivalence, overwrite refusal, failed-capacity preservation, partial-pair/work-path refusal, corrupt binary/mapping rejection and relocation of the two files. Packing regressions cover all four channel counts and exact guarded bytes. The independent metadata importer tests cover standalone and bunch publication, readable metadata mismatches, missing/mismatched pairs and rejected retired formats. The recorded supersampled-outline comparison established the scale qualification above; its former unit target is no longer part of the current suites. UI smoke and Testbed exercise the cooked default pair through the normal asset pipeline.

Windows ARM64 builds and tests can run on the current host. Changed Linux code can be checked with the Linux x86_64 syntax harness, but that does not establish native Linux linking, cross-platform bake byte equality, or compositor behavior. Live HDR presentation and other fonts' visual quality remain separate qualification work.
